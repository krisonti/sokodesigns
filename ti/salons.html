    How a salon gets permitted

    City first, State Board second. We draw for both at once.

    A salon has two inspectors who can stop you from opening: the city, who checks the building, and the Arizona Board of Barbering & Cosmetology, who licenses the shop. A set drawn for one and not the other is how salons lose a month at the finish line.

    What the city and the Board will ask for

    The six things that generate salon corrections.

    Every one of these is drawn, sized, and detailed before the set goes in. That's the difference between a first-submittal approval and chairs sitting in boxes.

    Shampoo bowls & pedicure chairs

    Each one is a drain, a trap, and hot water. Piped pedicure chairs are a plumbing fixture; pipeless ones still need power and a drain nearby.

    Plumbing

    Water heater sizing

    Four bowls running at once empties a residential tank in minutes. We size the heater to the bowls, and the city checks the math.

    Plumbing

    Ventilation

    Nail stations need source-capture exhaust under the mechanical code. Dryers and steam rooms need makeup air. Both are on the mechanical sheet.

    Mechanical

    Electrical

    Hot tools, dryers, and sterilizers on dedicated 20-amp circuits, GFCI near water, and panel capacity checked before you order equipment.

    Electrical

    Accessibility

    Accessible route, restroom, and at least one accessible station and shampoo position. Reviewers look for it on every salon set.

    ADA

    State Board & suites

    The Arizona Board of Barbering & Cosmetology licenses the salon after the city. Independent suites each need their own license and demising, which changes the layout.
