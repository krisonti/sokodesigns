    What the city will ask for

    The six things that generate office corrections.

    Every one of these is drawn and resolved before the set goes in. That's the difference between a first-submittal approval and a month of back-and-forth while your team works from a coffee shop.

    Demising & rated walls

    Walls between tenants and corridors usually need a fire rating, and that rating has to continue above the ceiling. Drawn with the right assembly from the start.

    Building

    Restroom count & accessibility

    Occupant load sets the fixture count. Accessibility sets the clearances and the route from the entry. Both are checked on every office TI.

    Plumbing + ADA

    Exits & travel distance

    Over 49 occupants means two exits, and every private office adds to the path a reviewer measures. We lay out around it.

    Life safety

    Sprinklers & alarm

    New walls under existing heads mean head relocations, and new rooms need alarm coverage. A deferred submittal, coordinated so it doesn't stall the main permit.

    Fire

    HVAC zoning & power

    Conference rooms and server closets need their own zones and circuits. Base-building capacity is checked before you commit to a layout.

    Mechanical + electrical

    Change of use

    Retail to office, or office to medical-adjacent, changes the occupant load and sometimes the parking. We flag it in the first conversation.
