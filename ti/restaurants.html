    How a restaurant gets permitted

    Six reviews. We run them side by side.

    A restaurant isn't one permit. It's a building permit plus a health review plus a fire review plus a grease approval, and usually a liquor floor plan and a sign. Done in sequence, that's months. Done in parallel from one coordinated set, it's weeks.

    What the city will ask for

    The six things that generate restaurant corrections.

    Every one of these is drawn, sized, and detailed in the set before it goes in. That's the difference between a first-submittal approval and a three-round fight.

    Hood & exhaust

    Type I hood length, exhaust and makeup air, duct routing to the roof, and the suppression system the fire department signs off on.

    Mechanical + fire

    Grease interceptor

    Sized to your fixtures and approved through the city's pretreatment program. Phoenix in particular wants this right before plumbing rough-in.

    Plumbing

    Occupant load & exits

    Seat count drives exit widths, exit count, restroom fixtures, and sometimes sprinklers. We set it first so nothing downstream moves.

    Life safety

    Accessibility

    Accessible route, restrooms, counter heights, and seating. A change of occupancy usually triggers an upgrade, and the reviewer will look for it.

    ADA / 2010 Standards

    Health department layout

    Hand sinks, three-compartment and mop sinks, finishes, and equipment spacing per Maricopa County's food code. Reviewed from the same set.

    MCESD

    Liquor floor plan & sign

    The Arizona DLLC license wants a floor plan with the premises outlined. The sign permit is separate. Both come straight off the set.
