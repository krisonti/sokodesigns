    How a warehouse gets permitted

    Two reviewers, filed together.

    A warehouse build-out is a building permit and, more often than tenants expect, a fire department package for the racking. Filed in sequence, the second waits on the first. Filed together from one coordinated set, they run side by side.

    1

    Building check

    Sprinkler system, clear height, dock doors, power, and the occupancy the building was built for.

    2

    Layout

    Racking, aisles, office, mezzanine, exits across the floor. You approve it before details.

    3

    Drawings

    Floor plan, office build-out, MEP, structural for mezzanine and racking, exit signage.

    4

    City permit

    Submitted through your city's portal. We answer every correction.

    5

    Fire package

    High-piled storage and racking review, filed at the same time as the city.

    6

    Operate

    Inspections, Certificate of Occupancy, forklifts rolling.

    What the city and fire department will ask for

    The six things that generate warehouse corrections.

    Every one of these is classified, drawn, and detailed before the set goes in. That's the difference between a first-submittal approval and inventory sitting in a trailer.

    High-piled storage

    Combustible storage above 12 feet means a fire department review of commodity class, aisle widths, sprinkler density, and smoke venting. Its own permit, run alongside the building permit.

    Fire code

    Racking engineering

    Seismic anchorage, load plaques, and engineered drawings for the uprights, even under 12 feet in most Valley cities.

    Mezzanines

    Structural engineering, stairs and guards, egress from the platform, and the one-third floor-area rule. Exceed it and it's a second story with sprinkler consequences.

    Structural + egress

    Office inside the warehouse

    Business occupancy inside storage or factory space: separation, restrooms for the headcount, HVAC for the conditioned area, and an accessible route from parking.

    Occupancy

    Docks, doors & exits

    Exit access across a racked floor, exit signage, extinguisher placement, and dock changes that touch the structure or the fire lane.

    Life safety

    Hazardous materials & process

    Paint, solvents, compressed gas, welding, or lithium batteries move the space from storage to factory or hazardous occupancy and bring maximum-allowable-quantity rules.
