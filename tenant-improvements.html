What we prepare

A set the plan reviewer can approve.

Commercial review is stricter than residential and the comment cycles are longer. The fix is a complete first submittal — which is mostly about knowing what that jurisdiction asks for before you send it.

Life safety and egress plans — occupant load, exit widths, travel distance
ADA compliance — restrooms, clearances, approach, path of travel
Floor plans and demo plans for the build-out
Reflected ceiling, power, and finish plans as required
Occupancy classification and change-of-use documentation
Mechanical coordination including Manual J/S/D where it applies
Health department plan sets for food service
Architect or engineer stamps coordinated when your jurisdiction requires them
The real cost

The permit isn't the expensive part. The delay is.

Most TI projects don't get held up by a design problem. They get held up by a set that was missing something, and by nobody watching the submittal while the rent clock runs.

Correction cycles compound

Each round back through review can add weeks. A set built for the jurisdiction the first time is the single biggest lever on your open date.

Change of use is its own project

Turning retail into a restaurant, or office into a clinic, can trigger occupancy, egress, ADA, and grease or ventilation requirements that weren't there before. We flag that before you sign, not after.

Somebody has to chase it

We track the submittal through the portal and respond to comments directly. You get status updates instead of having to learn the city's system yourself.
