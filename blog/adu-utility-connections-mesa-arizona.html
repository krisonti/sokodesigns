How Your ADU Connects to Sewer, Water & Electric in Mesa (and What It Costs)

By Kris OntiverosSeptember 27, 20267 min read

Mesa gives ADU owners more choice on utilities than any other Valley city: the ordinance says a casita may have its own meters or submeters for water, sewer, and electric, with permits. That flexibility is real, but one line in the City's fee schedule makes the decision for most people. Here is how each connection works on a Mesa lot, what we verify, and what it costs.

The short version

In Mesa, your ADU ties into the water and sewer service your home already has, and gets its own electric meter from SRP or, in the downtown service area, City of Mesa Electric. A separate City water meter is allowed but rarely worth it, because a new or upsized Mesa meter triggers the City's water and wastewater capacity fee.

Sewer
Tie into your home's existing sewer line. Same provider as the house is required; sharing the lateral is standard.
Water
Tie into your home's water line, after the meter. A separate City meter is allowed, but it carries a capacity fee. Submeter optional.
Electric
New, separate meter for the ADU. SRP in most of Mesa; City of Mesa Electric in the downtown core.

Distance from your home's lines to the ADU is still the biggest driver of utility cost. We route trenches as short as the lot allows, and we confirm your electric provider by address before anything is drawn.

What Mesa's ordinance actually says

Mesa's zoning ordinance requires three things of every ADU: it must be provided with water, sewer, and electric; it must be served by the same utility provider as the primary residence; and it can't sit on a public utility easement without written consent from the utilities that use it (City of Mesa ADU page). The City's ADU FAQ adds that an ADU "may have separate meters or sub-meters for water, sewer, and electric utilities, but permits are required." Mesa's first ADU ordinance in 2024 required the same meters as the house; the current version dropped that, so the choice is now yours.

Sewer: one line, one account

Your ADU connects to your home's existing sewer line, not to a new city tap. Mesa's utility terms require each parcel to be served from its own connection to the public main, and the parcel is the unit, not the building, so the casita sharing the house's lateral is the normal arrangement. Sewer in Mesa is billed off the water meter, so there is no separate sewer account unless there's a separate water meter.

What we verify at the site visit:

  • Grade. Sewer flows by gravity. If the ADU sits lower than your home's line, a small ejector pump may be needed.
  • Pipe size and condition. We confirm a 4-inch line. Older homes may get a camera inspection first; damaged pipe is replaced as a change order.
  • Easements. Mesa doesn't allow a detached accessory structure to encroach on any recorded easement, so we pull the plat before we place the unit.
  • Septic lots. If your home is on septic, Maricopa County Environmental Services reviews the system for the added load before the City will issue a permit.

Water: share the meter, skip the capacity fee

Your ADU tees off your home's water line after the existing City meter, with its own shutoff valve. Mesa will set a separate meter for an ADU if you ask, but since January 2026 a new or upsized Mesa water meter triggers the City's water and wastewater capacity fee, a one-time charge that runs into several thousand dollars for even a 3/4-inch meter, on top of the meter installation fee. That is why the shared line is our standard, and why we run the fixture count early: if your existing meter is adequate for the house plus the ADU, no upsize and no capacity fee.

  • Meter size check. We calculate the combined fixture load of your home plus the ADU. If your current meter can't carry it, an upsize is required, and the capacity fee applies to the difference in size.
  • Optional private submeter. Planning to rent the ADU? A submeter on the ADU's line lets you track its water use and bill a tenant without a second City account. Arizona's tenant-billing rules are in our separate meter vs. submeter guide.

Electric: SRP or City of Mesa, its own meter

Most of Mesa is SRP territory. Roughly five and a half square miles around downtown are served by City of Mesa Electric instead, and the ordinance requires the ADU to use the same provider as the house, so there's no choosing between them. Either way, the ADU gets its own meter, its own bill, and adds no load to your home's panel.

  • SRP areas. SRP supplies one service per building, and a detached casita is its own building. SOKO applies, SRP sets the connection point, we trench and install conduit to their design, and SRP pulls wire and sets the meter.
  • City of Mesa Electric areas. The City quotes connection and metering facilities per project. We request the quote as part of design so it lands in your proposal, not as a surprise.
  • Address. A separate meter needs its own address. Mesa Development Services assigns it at permit, and SOKO handles the request.
  • Lead time. Meter scheduling is set by the utility, not SOKO.

Option: power from your home's panel

Mesa allows the ADU to share the house's electric service, so we can instead feed it from your home's panel on one bill when a load calculation shows room, usually a 200-amp service. It's often a fit for family or guest use, and if you have rooftop solar it's usually the better economics. If your panel is too small, a new ADU meter is usually better value than upgrading your home's service.

What moves the number

  • Distance. Every foot between your home's lines and the ADU is priced. We place the ADU and route trenches to keep runs short.
  • Your water meter. An adequate existing meter keeps you clear of the capacity fee. An undersized one is the biggest fee swing on a Mesa project.
  • Your electric provider. SRP connections follow a published process; City of Mesa Electric connections are quoted per project.
  • Soil and existing conditions. Caliche, old clay sewer pipe, and shallow laterals in older central Mesa neighborhoods add scope.

What this pricing assumes

  • Pricing applies to City of Mesa projects. For other cities see our posts for Phoenix, Scottsdale, Tempe, Chandler, Gilbert, and unincorporated Maricopa County.
  • Run lengths are measured at the site visit and confirmed on the permit site plan.
  • Utility and City fees are billed at cost, as invoiced.
  • Excludes gas service (Mesa's municipal gas or Southwest Gas), relocation of existing utilities, landscape and hardscape restoration beyond the trench, and repairs to existing home systems.
  • Requirements reflect Mesa Zoning Ordinance Section 11-31-3 and City utility policies as of September 2026 and may change.
