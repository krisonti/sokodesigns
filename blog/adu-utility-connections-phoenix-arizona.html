How Your ADU Connects to Sewer, Water & Electric in Phoenix (and What It Costs)

By Kris OntiverosSeptember 27, 20267 min read

Every casita needs three things before anyone can live in it: somewhere for the water to come from, somewhere for it to go, and power. In the City of Phoenix, the rules for all three are unusually clear, and they happen to be the money-saving version. Here is exactly how your ADU gets connected, what we verify on your lot, and what each piece costs.

The short version

In Phoenix, your ADU shares the sewer and water service your home already has, and gets its own electric meter. That keeps connection costs down and gives the unit its own power bill from day one.

Sewer
Tie into your home's existing sewer line. Required by the City of Phoenix. No new street tap.
Water
Tie into your home's water line, after the meter. The City allows one water meter per lot for an ADU project.
Electric
New, separate SRP or APS meter for the ADU. Separate bill, no load added to your home's panel.

We keep every trench run as short as the site allows. Distance from your home's lines to the ADU is the single biggest driver of utility cost, which is why utility routing is part of where we place the unit, not an afterthought.

Sewer: one line, one account

Your ADU connects to your home's existing sewer line, not to a new city tap. Phoenix Water Services policy requires ADUs to share the home's sewer service, and the shared connection is still billed as one single-family account. That is spelled out in the City's ADU water and wastewater policy. A separate tap is only entertained through a technical appeal when sharing genuinely isn't feasible, and on a normal residential lot it almost always is.

What we verify at the site visit:

  • Grade. Sewer flows by gravity. If the ADU sits lower than your home's line, a small ejector pump may be needed.
  • Pipe size and condition. We confirm a 4-inch line. Older homes may get a camera inspection first; damaged clay pipe is replaced as a change order.
  • Easements. The ADU can't be built over a public utility easement without the utility's written consent.
  • Septic lots. If your home is on septic, Maricopa County must confirm the tank can take the extra flow.

Water: tee off after your meter

Your ADU tees off your home's water line after the existing city meter, with its own shutoff valve. Phoenix allows one water meter connection per lot for an ADU project, so a second city meter from the street is not the standard path (see the City of Phoenix ADU process).

  • Meter size check. We calculate the combined fixture load of your home plus the ADU. If your current meter is too small, the City requires an upsize, and new taps must be at least 1 inch (City meter sizing guideline).
  • Optional private submeter. Planning to rent the ADU? A submeter on the ADU's line lets you track its water use and bill a tenant. The City doesn't read it; you do. It's an add-on, installed during plumbing rough-in. Arizona's rules for billing a tenant off a submeter are covered in our separate meter vs. submeter guide.

Electric: its own meter, its own bill

Your ADU gets its own electric meter from SRP or APS, whichever serves your home. That means a separate power bill and no added load on your home's panel.

Standard: new, separate meter

  • SOKO applies to SRP or APS, and the utility sets the connection point.
  • SOKO digs the trench and installs conduit to the utility's design; the utility pulls the wire and sets the meter (SRP electric service specifications).
  • A separate meter needs its own address. SOKO requests it from City of Phoenix Addressing Services.
  • Plan on added utility lead time. Scheduling is set by SRP or APS, not SOKO.

Option: power from your home's panel

We can instead feed the ADU from your home's electrical panel, with one shared bill. This works when a load calculation shows your panel has room, usually a 200-amp service. It's often a fit for family or guest use, and if you have rooftop solar it's usually the better economics. If your panel is too small, a new ADU meter is usually better value than upgrading your home's service.

What moves the number

  • Distance. Every foot between your home's lines and the ADU is priced. We place the ADU and route trenches to keep runs short.
  • Soil. Caliche and rock are common in Phoenix and slow excavation.
  • Existing conditions. Old or damaged sewer pipe, a shallow sewer line, or an undersized water meter add scope.
  • Utility requirements. SRP or APS sets the meter location and service route, which can lengthen the electric trench.

What this pricing assumes

  • Pricing applies to City of Phoenix projects. Other Valley cities handle water and sewer differently; see our city-by-city posts for Scottsdale, Mesa, Tempe, Chandler, Gilbert, and unincorporated Maricopa County.
  • Run lengths are measured at the site visit and confirmed on the permit site plan.
  • Utility and City fees are billed at cost, as invoiced.
  • Excludes relocation of existing utilities, gas service, landscape and hardscape restoration beyond the trench, and repairs to existing home systems.
  • Requirements reflect published City of Phoenix and utility policies as of September 2026 and may change.
